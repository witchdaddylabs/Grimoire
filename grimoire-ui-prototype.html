Mara Thorne speaks like someone who learned early that every word can be used against her. She is restrained by instinct, not shyness. When she is angry, her sentences become shorter. When she is afraid, she becomes polite.

She remembers Vel Ashen by smell before sight: river silt, smoke, old stone, and the metallic wetness that gathers under the bridges before dawn. She does not romanticise the city. She survived it.

Her rule is simple: never explain pain to someone who benefits from misunderstanding it.
